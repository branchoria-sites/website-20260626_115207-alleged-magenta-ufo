## Introduction

The idea that Giuseppe Belluzzo's **Turboproietti** somehow explained the alleged 1933 Magenta UFO crash occupies an important but often misunderstood place in [later retellings]({{ 'later-retellings/' | relative_url }}) of the story. It does not provide evidence that an unidentified craft crashed near Magenta. Instead, it illustrates how genuine wartime engineering concepts became woven into a much larger mythology about flying discs, secret Axis research and, eventually, alleged extraterrestrial technology.

Within Magenta narratives, Belluzzo's work functions primarily as a bridge. A real engineer with recognised expertise in turbines and wartime technology proposed unusual circular aircraft concepts after the Second World War. Later authors used those concepts to suggest that Fascist Italy—or later Nazi Germany—may have been developing disc-shaped aircraft inspired by a recovered object. The historical record, however, does not establish such a connection, and the leap from unconventional engineering to crash-retrieval evidence remains speculative rather than documentary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist ...August 10, 2020 — 10 Aug 2020 — Another question relates to the original idea of th...</span></span></span>

## Did Belluzzo's Disc Designs Explain Magenta?

The short answer is no. Belluzzo's circular aircraft ideas demonstrate that Axis engineers explored unconventional aerodynamic and propulsion concepts, but they do not independently support the claim that a UFO crashed near Magenta in 1933.

The importance of the Turboproietti lies less in what it proves than in how it has been used. Modern Magenta accounts frequently point to Belluzzo as evidence that Italy possessed advanced circular aircraft research. They then imply that this research may have originated from [reverse engineering]({{ 'reverse-engineering/' | relative_url }}) of a recovered craft. The historical evidence supports the first proposition only in a limited sense: Belluzzo publicly discussed circular aircraft concepts after the war. It does not support the second proposition, because no verified documentation links his engineering work to any alleged 1933 recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist ...August 10, 2020 — 10 Aug 2020 — Another question relates to the original idea of th...</span></span></span>

## What Belluzzo's Turboproietti Were Claimed to Be

Giuseppe Belluzzo was not an obscure figure. He was a distinguished Italian engineer known for his work on turbines and held senior political positions during the Fascist period before returning to engineering and technical writing after the war. In March 1950 he published an article arguing that "flying saucers" had been conceived as military projects in Italy and Germany during the Second World War rather than being spacecraft from another world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Belluzzo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Giuseppe Belluzzo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Giuseppe Belluzzo</span></span></span>

According to Belluzzo's later descriptions and subsequent reconstructions:

* the **Turboproietti** ("projectile turbine") was conceived as a circular, jet-propelled device;
* propulsion would come from jet pipes arranged around the perimeter of an annular structure;
* the design emphasised rotational motion and high-speed airflow around the rim;
* contemporary descriptions suggest Belluzzo envisioned it primarily as an unmanned weapon rather than a crewed flying saucer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist ...August 10, 2020 — 10 Aug 2020 — Another question relates to the original idea of th...</span></span></span>

Several later technical reconstructions describe a turbine feeding peripheral jet pipes whose combustion would accelerate airflow around the disc. These descriptions largely derive from later secondary interpretations rather than surviving engineering documentation or flight-test records. More importantly, there is no evidence that a full-scale Turboproietti prototype ever flew. Even sympathetic accounts generally acknowledge that the concept remained experimental.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault+2Nevington War Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist ...August 10, 2020 — 10 Aug 2020 — Another question relates to the original idea of th...</span></span></span>

## Why Circular Aircraft Are Not Crash Evidence

The presence of genuine circular aircraft proposals does not transform the Magenta story into a documented historical event.

Military [aviation]({{ 'aviation/' | relative_url }}) between the 1930s and 1950s produced many unconventional concepts. Engineers investigated flying wings, annular wings, tail-sitters, ramjets, pulse-jets and numerous circular or near-circular lifting bodies. Most never entered operational service because stability, control and propulsion proved exceptionally difficult.

Belluzzo's work belongs naturally within that broader history of experimental aeronautics rather than outside it. The fact that an engineer explored a disc-shaped vehicle demonstrates only that unusual aircraft concepts were considered. It does not establish:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* that such vehicles were successfully built;
* that they resembled alleged UFO recoveries;
* that they originated from non-human technology;
* or that they were connected with an alleged crash near Magenta seventeen years earlier.

</div>

This distinction is frequently blurred in later UFO literature. Once readers encounter a real historical engineer associated with circular aircraft, the engineering concept can appear to validate an otherwise unsupported narrative. In reality, the two lines of evidence remain separate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist ...August 10, 2020 — 10 Aug 2020 — Another question relates to the original idea of th...</span></span></span>

## How the SS Connection Changes the Story's Tone

One reason Belluzzo attracts attention in Magenta discussions is the claim that he worked with German SS organisations after Italy's 1943 armistice.

Several UFO-oriented sources state that Belluzzo continued technical work under German supervision and developed versions of the Turboproietti or related circular weapons projects during the closing stages of the war. These accounts often mention associated concepts such as unmanned "Flakmine" variants and collaborations with German engineers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist ...August 10, 2020 — 10 Aug 2020 — Another question relates to the original idea of th...</span></span></span>

This association significantly changes the emotional tone of the Magenta narrative. Instead of remaining a story centred on Fascist Italy, it becomes linked with the wider mythology of Nazi secret weapons, hidden research facilities and post-war technological transfers. That narrative expansion is powerful because Germany genuinely developed advanced rockets, jet aircraft and other innovative weapons.

However, the evidential standard does not change. Even if Belluzzo participated in German wartime projects, no verified archive demonstrates that those activities originated from a recovered UFO or from material allegedly retrieved at Magenta. ## Why the Flying-Disc Leap Became So Attractive

Belluzzo's work occupies a unique position because it combines three ingredients that later authors found difficult to resist.

First, Belluzzo was a genuine engineer with recognised technical credentials rather than an anonymous witness.

Second, he publicly discussed circular aircraft at precisely the moment "flying saucers" became an international cultural phenomenon in the late 1940s and early 1950s. His argument was not that saucers were alien spacecraft, but that similar-looking machines could have originated from secret military research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Belluzzo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Giuseppe Belluzzo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Giuseppe Belluzzo</span></span></span>

Third, later writers connected his designs to broader stories involving German disc projects, alleged SS research and eventually the Magenta recovery narrative. Each additional link made the overall story appear more coherent, even though the supporting evidence for each connection remained limited.

The result is what might be called the "flying-disc leap": moving from a documented history of unusual aircraft concepts to an undocumented conclusion that those concepts originated from recovered extraterrestrial technology. Belluzzo's Turboproietti illustrates how easily authentic engineering history can be incorporated into UFO folklore without supplying independent proof for its central claims.
