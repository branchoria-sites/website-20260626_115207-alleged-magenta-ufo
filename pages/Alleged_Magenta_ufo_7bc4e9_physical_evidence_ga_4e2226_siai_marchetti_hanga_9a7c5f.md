## Introduction

The claim that the alleged 1933 Magenta UFO was hidden in the [SIAI-Marchetti]({{ 'siai/' | relative_url }}) aircraft works at Vergiate is one of the most concrete elements of the wider story because it names a real industrial site rather than an abstract secret location. That specificity, however, should not be mistaken for evidence. A genuine storage operation at a major aviation factory would be expected to leave administrative, engineering, security or personal traces. To date, no independently verified [hangar records]({{ 'hangar-records/' | relative_url }}), photographs, inventories or first-hand employee testimony have emerged that confirm an unusual object was stored there. ## Why the named facility matters

SIAI-Marchetti (originally Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia) was a genuine Italian aerospace company with major production facilities at [Vergiate]({{ 'vergiate/' | relative_url }}) in [Lombardy]({{ 'lombardy/' | relative_url }}). During the Fascist period it manufactured military aircraft for the Regia Aeronautica and was an important strategic industrial site. Because it already possessed large hangars, skilled engineers and military connections, it is plausible that any highly secret aeronautical project—if one existed—could have been taken there.

This real historical setting is why the Vergiate claim has remained influential within the Magenta narrative. Roberto Pinotti and Alfredo Lissoni argued that the recovered object was hidden in SIAI-Marchetti hangars after the alleged crash, with the supposed RS/33 committee studying it under Fascist authority. Later versions of the story added further claims that Allied forces reached the facility in 1945 and that the object was eventually transferred to the United States. These assertions originate from the same body of anonymously sourced "[Fascist UFO Files]({{ 'fascist-files/' | relative_url }})" rather than from independently located company or government archives. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the ufo files of mussolini fascist ufo files by roberto pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Later the crashed UFO was stored in the hangars of the SIAI Marchetti in Vergiate. The distinction is important. Naming a real factory can strengthen a narrative psychologically because readers can identify the place on a map and verify that it existed. It does not, by itself, demonstrate that the alleged events occurred there.

## What records should exist if an object were stored there?

A long-term storage operation involving an unknown aircraft at an active aviation factory would normally generate several categories of evidence beyond a single secret order.

Among the records historians would expect are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* transport documentation moving an oversized object to Vergiate;
* military security instructions assigning guards or restricting access;
* hangar allocation or facility maintenance records;
* engineering reports describing examination or measurements;
* procurement requests for unusual lifting or handling equipment;
* employee diaries, memoirs or interviews describing extraordinary security;
* wartime photographs showing unusual activity;
* surviving inventories after the factory changed ownership or administration.

</div>

None of these categories has been publicly produced in a verifiable form for the alleged Magenta object. The absence does not prove the story impossible—archives can be destroyed, classified or lost—but it does mean the storage claim lacks the corroborating infrastructure usually associated with an industrial operation of this scale. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2023/06/david-grush-and-1933-italian-ufo-crash.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">david grush and 1933 italian ufo crash</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin RandleDavid Grusch and the 1933 Italian UFO Crash21 Jun 2023 — The trouble there, according to an Italian researcher, Giuseppe Stil...</span></span></span>

## The evidence actually presented for Vergiate

The public case for Vergiate relies primarily on inference rather than direct documentation.

Pinotti's published account proposes the facility because it was nearby, strategically important and controlled by the Italian aviation establishment. Some versions argue that telegram traffic originated from Milan, that Blackshirt forces reportedly appeared in the area, or that aviation officials connected with SIAI-Marchetti had relationships with senior Fascist figures. These are presented as circumstantial indicators rather than documentary proof that a recovered craft entered a specific hangar. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the ufo files of mussolini fascist ufo files by roberto pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Later the crashed UFO was stored in the hangars of the SIAI Marchetti in Vergiate. [Later retellings]({{ 'later-retellings/' | relative_url }}) expanded the story further by linking the facility to a 1940 hangar fire or to Allied occupation in 1945. However, publicly available evidence has not established that either event involved an unidentified object. The association comes from later narrative development rather than contemporary company records demonstrating that an extraordinary item was present. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wantedinrome.com/news/the-crash-that-predated-roswell-mussolinis-secret-ufo-files.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wantedinrome.com">[Wanted in Rome]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wantedinrome.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wanted in RomeThe Secret Crash That Predated Roswell: Mussolini&#x27;s UFO...2 Mar 2026 — Italy&#x27;s Secret UFO Files: The Crash That Predated R...</span></span></span>

Crucially, no authenticated SIAI-Marchetti internal document has surfaced stating that an unknown aircraft, unconventional vehicle or similarly described object was received or stored at Vergiate.

## The missing company archive trail

The strongest support for the Vergiate claim would not necessarily be a dramatic confession but an ordinary business record.

Aircraft manufacturers routinely generated documentation covering production, storage, maintenance and security. Even highly classified military work typically left administrative traces, whether through accounting, personnel assignments or infrastructure planning. Historians investigating secret wartime programmes often reconstruct events precisely because these indirect records survive.

For the Magenta case, researchers have not identified:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* authenticated SIAI-Marchetti correspondence discussing an unidentified object;
* warehouse or hangar inventories referring to unusual cargo;
* engineering notebooks analysing non-standard materials;
* payroll or personnel records showing a special restricted project matching the alleged storage period.

</div>

Instead, the proposed storage location derives from later UFO literature based on anonymously delivered documents whose provenance remains disputed. Independent researchers, including Italian critics of the case, have repeatedly argued that the underlying documentary chain has not been established sufficiently to support historical conclusions. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2023/06/david-grush-and-1933-italian-ufo-crash.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">david grush and 1933 italian ufo crash</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin RandleDavid Grusch and the 1933 Italian UFO Crash21 Jun 2023 — The trouble there, according to an Italian researcher, Giuseppe Stil...</span></span></span>

## Why no employee testimony has emerged

Another notable gap concerns [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}).

A large industrial site employs engineers, mechanics, security personnel, administrators and maintenance workers. Even under strict secrecy, unusual operations often become known through recollections recorded decades later. Many historical covert programmes have eventually been reconstructed through interviews with former employees combined with surviving paperwork.

In the Vergiate case, no independently verified first-hand testimony from a SIAI-Marchetti employee has publicly confirmed seeing or guarding an anomalous craft in the relevant period. ## How a real place can lend false confidence

The Vergiate story illustrates an important principle in evaluating historical claims.

Specificity is not the same as verification. A narrative becomes more persuasive when it includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* a genuine company;
* a real airfield or factory;
* identifiable political figures;
* historically accurate geography.

</div>

These authentic details create a framework that feels concrete. Yet every central claim still requires independent evidence. A real hangar does not prove an extraordinary object was inside it, just as a real military base does not automatically validate every story associated with it.

For the Magenta case, the named location is therefore both its greatest strength and one of its greatest weaknesses. It offers investigators a defined place where corroborating evidence might reasonably exist, but decades of public discussion have not produced authenticated company records, contemporary photographs or verifiable employee testimony demonstrating that SIAI-Marchetti ever stored the alleged recovered craft.
