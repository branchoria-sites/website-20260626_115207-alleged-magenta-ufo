## Introduction

One of the most useful ways to evaluate [later additions]({{ 'later-additions/' | relative_url }}) to the alleged 1933 Magenta UFO story is not to ask whether they are dramatic or plausible, but whether they fit the internal rules of the documents they supposedly came from. Within Pepijn [van Erp]({{ 'van-erp/' | relative_url }})'s critique, the alleged Cabinet RS/33 protocol functions as an internal consistency test. If later claims contradict the protocol's own secrecy rules or administrative structure, they become harder to reconcile with the purported documentary trail, even before broader questions about authenticity are addressed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pepijnvanerp.nl/2023/06/mussolinis-ufo-cabinet-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pepijnvanerp.nl">[Pepijn van Erp]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pepijnvanerp.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pepijn van Erp Mussolini&#x27;s UFO – Cabinet RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How lines run between the various players in the modern UFO mythology is...Read more...</span></span></span>

This approach does not require proving that the RS/33 documents are genuine. Instead, it asks a narrower question: assuming the documents represent the original version of the story, do later embellishments preserve their logic, or do they introduce details that appear to originate elsewhere? That distinction makes the protocol valuable as a test of narrative continuity rather than as independent evidence that the alleged events occurred.

## What the protocol was supposed to control

According to Roberto Pinotti's published account of the alleged Fascist documents, one item is presented as a protocol governing how reports about unusual aerial objects were to be handled. The document describes an extremely centralised reporting process in which communications concerning anomalous aircraft would be channelled through the secret [Cabinet]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) RS/33 before reaching military authorities. It also specifies that other institutions were to be excluded from the reporting chain, including the Pontifical University.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pepijnvanerp.nl/2023/06/mussolinis-ufo-cabinet-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pepijnvanerp.nl">[Pepijn van Erp]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pepijnvanerp.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pepijn van Erp Mussolini&#x27;s UFO – Cabinet RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How lines run between the various players in the modern UFO mythology is...Read more...</span></span></span>

Within the story's own framework, this alleged protocol serves several purposes:

* it establishes RS/33 as the sole coordinating authority;
* it limits dissemination of information;
* it emphasises bureaucratic control rather than broad scientific consultation;

Whether authentic or forged, those rules define the internal logic of the surviving papers. Any later claim that depends on information flowing outside those channels therefore needs to explain why the protocol would not apply.

## Which later claims strain the chain of custody

Van Erp argues that the strongest example is the modern claim that the [Vatican]({{ 'vatican/' | relative_url }}), through Pope Pius XII, secretly informed American intelligence about the recovered craft during the Second World War. This element became widely known after David Grusch discussed the alleged Italian recovery in 2023.

The difficulty is not merely that independent evidence for the Vatican story is lacking. The problem is that the alleged RS/33 protocol itself appears to forbid exactly the sort of institutional involvement that the later narrative requires. If reports were meant to remain under RS/33 control while excluding outside organisations—including the Pontifical University—the later image of the Vatican acting as an intermediary sits awkwardly with the surviving document set.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pepijnvanerp.nl/2023/06/mussolinis-ufo-cabinet-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pepijnvanerp.nl">[Pepijn van Erp]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pepijnvanerp.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pepijn van Erp Mussolini&#x27;s UFO – Cabinet RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How lines run between the various players in the modern UFO mythology is...Read more...</span></span></span>

Van Erp therefore treats the contradiction as evidence of narrative layering rather than confirmation of a hidden historical event. His suggestion is that the Vatican element may have entered the story through later retellings instead of being inherited from the original document collection.

A similar reasoning applies to the later description of the object as a "bell-shaped" craft. Van Erp notes that he could not locate this description in the early Italian sources associated with Pinotti. Instead, he traces the terminology to later UFO literature, particularly accounts connected with Billy Brophy. In this reading, both the Vatican role and the craft's shape emerge outside the earliest documentary layer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pepijnvanerp.nl/2023/06/mussolinis-ufo-cabinet-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pepijnvanerp.nl">[Pepijn van Erp]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pepijnvanerp.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pepijn van Erp Mussolini&#x27;s UFO – Cabinet RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How lines run between the various players in the modern UFO mythology is...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why internal fit matters before belief

The RS/33 protocol illustrates a broader historical principle. Before asking whether a document is genuine, historians often ask whether successive versions of a story remain internally coherent.

An internally consistent archive is not automatically authentic. Forged documents can also be carefully constructed. However, when later claims directly contradict the operational rules described in the documents they supposedly derive from, confidence usually decreases rather than increases.

Applied to the Magenta case, the sequence looks like this:

## Why the protocol is a stronger test than dramatic details

The protocol has particular value because it concerns administrative procedure rather than sensational claims. Descriptions of craft shape, recovered occupants or intelligence operations can evolve through repeated retelling, memory and popular UFO traditions. Bureaucratic procedures, by contrast, tend to constrain how a narrative is supposed to function.

If a story claims that every report had to pass through one secret office while excluding outside bodies, then later accounts involving independent religious, diplomatic or foreign intermediaries require additional evidence. Without that evidence, they appear less like recovered historical information and more like narrative expansion.

This is one reason Van Erp gives the protocol greater analytical weight than later eyewitness-style details. Rather than debating whether an extraordinary event happened, he examines whether the evolving story still obeys its own documentary rules.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pepijnvanerp.nl/2023/06/mussolinis-ufo-cabinet-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pepijnvanerp.nl">[Pepijn van Erp]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pepijnvanerp.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pepijn van Erp Mussolini&#x27;s UFO – Cabinet RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How lines run between the various players in the modern UFO mythology is...Read more...</span></span></span>

## The significance for assessing later embellishment

Within the debate over the alleged Magenta crash, the RS/33 protocol is best understood as a consistency check rather than proof for either side. Believers may regard the protocol as evidence that a sophisticated secrecy system existed, while [sceptics]({{ 'sceptics/' | relative_url }}) often question the authenticity of the documents themselves. Van Erp's contribution occupies a narrower position: even if one temporarily accepts the documents as the baseline narrative, later additions should still be expected to match their stated procedures.

By that standard, the protocol becomes an embellishment test. Claims that remain compatible with the original chain of custody are at least internally coherent.
